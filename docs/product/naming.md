# Name and logo: where it stands

Status: **Intus** is chosen (6 Oct 2026). The domain `intus.fit` is bought and connected to Vercel, and the handle `@getintus` is secured on Twitter and Instagram. Still open: app store search and a trademark clearance search for Intus (filters 2 and 3 below), and the logo. The shortlist below is the earlier work and is kept for the record.

Where the name is applied: the in-app wordmark, page title, footer, coming-soon screen and README. The GitHub repo and the Vercel project are both called `intus`. Notion, Miro and Figma still carry "Health OS".

## Positioning (draft, from the PRD)

One line: the honest health hub. Wearable and bloodwork data in one place, scores that show their working, AI that never invents numbers, your data kept yours, on iOS and Android, at a simple price.

Three adjectives the name should feel like: plain-spoken, rigorous, calm.

What it must not feel like: a strap, a ring, a "bio-hack", a clinic, or a copy of Bevel, Whoop or Oura. Whoop is already suing Bevel over trademark, patents and copyright, so keep clear of both names and of anything that rhymes with them.

## What the evidence says

I checked DNS from this environment for 44 plain words and 63 compounds and coined words, on `.com`, `.app`, `.health` and `.co`. A name that resolves is registered (it may be parked or for sale). A name that does not resolve is only unknown: it can still be registered and unused, and DNS says nothing about trademarks or app store names.

- 43 of the 44 plain words are registered on `.com`. That includes both seed ideas: Cairn and Baseline resolve on all four TLDs. So do Candor, Plumb, Waymark, Ledger, Tally, Marker and Lucid, and 13 others.
- The one plain word with no `.com` answer is `groundwork`, which also has none on `.app`.
- Of the 63 compounds and coined words, only 9 had no `.com` answer, and 4 of those also had none on `.app`.

So the realistic options are a compound or coined word, a plain word with a modifier ("get", "use", "app", "health"), or paying for a parked domain.

My lean: Groundwork or Groundread, because the name itself repeats the product's one differentiating promise. That is a taste call, and the filters below can overturn it.

## Filters, in this order (cheap first)

1. **Registrar search** for the exact `.com` and `.app`. DNS only tells you "registered or unknown".
2. **App stores.** Search the exact name and near spellings in the App Store and Google Play. A health app with the same name is a deal-breaker.
3. **Trademark.** Search the exact name and near sounds in the registers of every market you will launch in, such as USPTO, WIPO Global Brand Database and your national office. Check class 9 (software), class 42 (software as a service) and class 44 (medical and health services). A lawyer's clearance search before spending on branding is worth the money.
4. **Handles.** The same name on Instagram, X, TikTok and Reddit. A trailing underscore or a "get" prefix is acceptable, a different word is not.
5. **Say it.** Say it on the phone, spell it back, and type it into search. If people have to be told how it is spelt, it fails.

## Logo, after the name, and the app icon first

The icon is the logo for most users: a 60 pixel square on a phone screen. Design it first and build the wordmark around it.

- One shape, one or two colours, readable at 29 pixels and in the dark and light themes.
- Avoid hearts, pulse lines, droplets, leaves and rings. They are the category's clichés, and a ring is Oura's.
- Concepts that fit "grounded, shows its working": a stacked line (baseline) with a single marker on it, a plumb-line shape, or a simple open circle with a measured tick. Test each as a black-and-white silhouette before adding any colour.
